How to Read Hangul Syllable Blocks

Korean writing is an alphabet arranged in blocks. Instead of reading every shape as a separate word, read one block as one syllable. Once you can see the initial consonant, vowel and optional final consonant, a word such as 한국 becomes much less intimidating.

In this lesson

  2. Look right or down for the vowel
  3. Notice silent initial ㅇ

  5. A five-minute practice routine

Find the consonant and vowel

Every written syllable needs an initial consonant position and a vowel. In 가, ㄱ comes first and ㅏ is the vowel. In 나, ㄴ combines with the same vowel. The letters keep their identities even though their positions change. Start with the sound of the whole syllable, then identify the parts.

Look right or down for the vowel

A vertical vowel such as ㅏ sits to the right: ㄱ + ㅏ → 가. A horizontal vowel such as ㅗ sits below: ㄱ + ㅗ → 고. More complex vowels can occupy both areas. These are layout rules, not instructions to pause between letters. Say 가 as one beat rather than a consonant followed by a separate English vowel.

Notice silent initial ㅇ

A syllable that begins with a vowel still needs the initial position filled. Initial ㅇ does this without adding a consonant sound: 아 starts with the vowel 아. Compare 아 with 나. At the bottom of a block, however, ㅇ represents an ng sound. Its position matters; it is not always silent.

Add a final consonant

In 한, ㅎ and ㅏ form the top portion and ㄴ sits below. Read 한 as one syllable. Then read 국 as another syllable: 한국 has two blocks and two syllables. The final consonant is called 받침. Do not add an extra vowel after it. You can practise without a final consonant first, then add one in the Hangul builder.

A five-minute practice routine

Read 가, 나 and 아 aloud. Change ㅏ to ㅗ to make 고, 노 and 오. Next compare 가 with 간: only the final ㄴ changes. Finally copy 한 and 국 while saying each whole block. Romanization can help you get started, but return your eyes to the Korean letters so that you learn to read rather than decode English spelling.

A common mistake

Do not read 한국 as three or four English-style syllables. Keep the final consonants attached to their blocks. Actual pronunciation can change when syllables meet; learning block structure is the first step, not the entire pronunciation system.

Quick self-check

Answer all three questions, then check your understanding. This is practice, not a proficiency certificate.

1. How many syllable blocks are in 한국?

한 and 국 are two blocks, each representing one syllable.

2. What does initial ㅇ do in 아?

Initial ㅇ is silent. Final ㅇ has a different job.

3. Where is ㄴ in 한?

ㄴ is the final consonant underneath ㅎ and ㅏ.
