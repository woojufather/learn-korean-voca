Korean Numbers for Prices, People and Drinks

Korean uses two common number systems. You do not need to master every number at once. Start by separating the numbers you hear in prices from the numbers used with everyday counters such as people and cups.

In this lesson

  1. Use Sino-Korean numbers for prices
  2. Build the amount in chunks
  3. Use native numbers with common counters
  4. Remember that context chooses the system
  5. Practise a real purchase
  6. Listen to examples

Use Sino-Korean numbers for prices

일, 이, 삼, 사, 오, 육, 칠, 팔, 구 and 십 are the basic Sino-Korean numbers from one to ten. Price amounts normally use this system with 원. 천 원 is 1,000 won, 이천 원 is 2,000 won and 오천 원 is 5,000 won. In 일천, the initial 일 is normally omitted in everyday prices: say 천 원.

Build the amount in chunks

십 is ten, 백 is one hundred and 천 is one thousand. 삼천오백 원 means 3,500 won: three thousand plus five hundred. Korean groups larger values around 만, ten thousand. Do not force a large price into English “thousand” groupings while listening. For a beginner, practising amounts below 만 first is a useful step.

Use native numbers with common counters

Native Korean numbers begin 하나, 둘, 셋 and 넷. Before many counters, these become 한, 두, 세 and 네. 한 명 is one person, 두 명 is two people and 세 잔 is three cups. The counter tells you what is being counted, so learn a number and its counter as a small phrase.

Remember that context chooses the system

Prices usually use Sino-Korean numbers, while people and cups commonly use native numbers. Time combines the systems: native numbers for hours and Sino-Korean numbers for minutes. Phone numbers and dates have their own common patterns too. Rather than guessing from the English number, identify the kind of information first.

Practise a real purchase

Ask 얼마예요? and listen for an amount ending in 원. Then order 두 잔 주세요 for two drinks. You have used two systems in one transaction. Write 2,000 and 2 cups on a note and say each aloud: 이천 원, 두 잔. This contrast makes the rule easier to remember than reciting both full number lists.

A common mistake

Do not say 둘 명 or 셋 잔 in ordinary counting phrases. Use 두 명 and 세 잔. Also avoid treating 만 as one thousand: it means ten thousand.

Quick self-check

Answer all three questions, then check your understanding. This is practice, not a proficiency certificate.

1. Which means 2,000 won?

이천 is two thousand.

2. Which is the usual phrase for two people?

둘 changes to 두 before 명.

3. What value is 만?

만 is ten thousand.
