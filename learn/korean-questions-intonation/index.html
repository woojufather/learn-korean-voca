Korean Questions and Statement Intonation

Korean can use the same everyday polite ending in a statement and a question. 가요. can say that someone goes, while 가요? can ask whether they go. Meaning comes from context and intonation as well as the words. Punctuation is a helpful reading cue, but real conversation is more flexible.

Compare a statement and a yes/no question

A statement such as 괜찮아요. usually settles toward its end. A neutral yes/no question such as 괜찮아요? often rises gently. Keep the beginning similar and let the end carry the contrast. Do not jump to a very high pitch or separate the last syllable from the rest of the word.

Do not make every question rise

Questions containing 뭐, 누구 or 어디 can have falling or other contours depending on context, emphasis and the speaker. 어디 가요? is still a question even when it does not sound like an exaggerated English yes/no question. Korean dialects and individual voices also differ. Listen to a whole exchange rather than applying one rule to every question mark.

Learn question words in a frame

뭐예요? means “What is it?” 어디예요? asks where something is. 언제 가요? asks when someone is going. Replace only the question word at first and notice how it changes the information being requested. Korean often leaves out the subject when the conversation already makes it clear.

Read punctuation but keep the phrase connected

Written periods and question marks help a speech system interpret a sentence. Browser-generated speech still depends on the installed Korean voice. A slower speed can make the words easier to hear, but it does not guarantee a perfect conversational contour. Compare available voices and use the app’s complete-phrase playback instead of spelling out syllables.

Practise with a partner or a recording

Say 있어요. as a statement, pause, then 있어요? as a question. Answer the question with 네, 있어요 or 아니요, 없어요. Repeat with 괜찮아요. and 괜찮아요? while imagining the situation. Recording yourself can help you hear the difference; automatic speech recognition checks recognized words and does not reliably grade the intonation itself.

A common mistake

A question mark does not mean “always raise the pitch sharply.” Avoid chopping off and resynthesizing the final syllable. Practise natural connected speech and keep the situation in mind.

Quick self-check

Answer all three questions, then check your understanding. This is practice, not a proficiency certificate.

1. A neutral yes/no question often…

A gentle rise is common, but context and voice matter.

2. Must every Korean question rise?

Question-word questions can fall depending on context.

3. Does speech recognition reliably grade intonation?

Recognized words do not provide a reliable intonation score.
