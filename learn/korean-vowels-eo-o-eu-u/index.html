Korean Vowels: ㅓ vs ㅗ and ㅡ vs ㅜ

English spelling cannot represent every Korean vowel accurately. Two useful contrasts are ㅓ versus ㅗ and ㅡ versus ㅜ. Work with your lips and tongue while listening to a Korean voice, rather than treating both pairs as the same English sound.

In this lesson

  1. Keep a vowel steady
  2. ㅓ and ㅗ: check your lips
  3. ㅡ and ㅜ: round or unround

Keep a vowel steady

Korean vowels are useful to practise as steady sounds. Avoid automatically turning 오 into the English glide in “go.” Say the vowel comfortably, keep the mouth position stable, and stop. A recording or a good Korean speech voice is a better reference than a romanized word alone.

ㅓ and ㅗ: check your lips

For ㅓ, represented by 어 on its own, keep the lips relatively unrounded and the mouth open. For ㅗ, represented by 오, round the lips. Tongue position also differs. The spelling eo represents one Korean vowel, not an e sound followed by an o sound. Alternate 어–오 slowly before adding consonants.

ㅡ and ㅜ: round or unround

For ㅡ, represented by 으, keep the lips unrounded. For ㅜ, represented by 우, round the lips. Both involve the tongue toward the back compared with 이, but do not force the mouth into an uncomfortable position. Compare 으–우 in a mirror, then try 그–구.

Move from isolated sounds to words

Compare 거 and 고, then 그 and 구. When a contrast becomes easier, listen to a short word and repeat it without exaggeration. Do not use pitch alone to distinguish the vowels: Korean does not use Mandarin-style lexical tones in standard Seoul speech. Mouth position and vowel quality are the important cues here.

Use a short feedback loop

Play one sound, pause, repeat it, and play it again. Practise two contrasting sounds in alternating order so that you do not simply memorize a sequence. If your device offers several Korean voices, compare them. Browser speech and speech recognition are practice aids; recognized words are not a reliable score for every detail of pronunciation.

Listen to useful examples

Listen, pause and repeat the complete phrase. Romanization is an approximate guide. The audio is device-generated Korean speech, not a native speaker recording.

  • 어eo

    One unrounded vowel, not two sounds.

  • 오o

    Round the lips; keep the vowel steady.

  • 으eu

    Keep the lips unrounded.

  • 우u

    Round the lips.

  • 그, 구geu, gu

    Compare the same consonant with two vowels.

A common mistake

Do not pronounce eo as “ee-oh” or eu as “you.” Romanization is a reading aid, not a precise pronunciation guide. A vowel contrast may take repeated listening over several days.

Quick self-check

Answer all three questions, then check your understanding. This is practice, not a proficiency certificate.

1. Which vowel normally uses rounded lips?

ㅗ is rounded; ㅓ and ㅡ are unrounded.

2. How many vowels does eo represent in 어?

eo is a romanization for one Korean vowel.

3. Which pair is a useful lip-rounding contrast?

ㅡ and ㅜ differ in lip rounding and vowel quality.
